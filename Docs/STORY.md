# Runes of Speech — The Story

A storyline to build quests and scenes from later. Nothing here is in the game yet except the places,
people and bosses it mentions. Each act lists what happens, who you meet, and the quests it would become.
Quest text will be in English; everything people say is in Spanish (with the globe to translate).

---

## The idea in one paragraph

Long ago the people of Valdeluz carved their words into runes, and the runes kept the valley alive: the
fountain flowed, the crops grew, and everyone understood each other. Then **El Rey Demonio** climbed out of
the dark under the mountain and stole the words, one kind at a time. He gave them to nine guardians and locked
them in **La Ciudadela del Sol**. Now the villagers are forgetting how to speak: first the names of things,
then how to say what was and what will be. You arrive knowing no Spanish at all, which is exactly why the
runes choose you: every word you learn is a word the Demon King never had the chance to steal.

**The rule of the world:** words are power. Learning a word re-carves a rune. That is why reading signs gives
XP, why spells are cast by spelling, and why bosses are beaten with right answers and not with swords.

---

## The people who matter

| Who | Role in the story |
|---|---|
| **You** | A stranger who arrives by the north road with nothing. The fountain's runes light up when you touch them. |
| **Sabia** (la sabia) | The last keeper of the runes. She remembers more words than anyone, but she is forgetting too. Your teacher. |
| **Abuela Rosa** | The village's heart. She takes you in. Her memory of her late husband's name is fading: the personal stake. |
| **Luna** (la niña) | Born after the theft. She has never heard the old songs. She follows you around asking what things are called. |
| **Doña Runa** (la encantadora) | Lives alone in the Rune Tower. She knows what the Demon King really is, and is afraid to say. |
| **Don Cortés** and **Sera y Estela** | Teachers at the Academy who kept grammar alive by drilling it every day. |
| **Mateo, Marina, Simón** | The fishing folk. They notice the river carries runes downstream from the citadel. |
| **Bruno** (el herrero) | Forges blades that only hold an edge if the right verb is spoken over them. |
| **Don Ernesto** and **Pepe** | Miners. They find relics from before the theft: proof the story is true. |
| **Inés** (la cazadora) | Guards the forest path. Her brother went up to the citadel and came back unable to speak. |
| **Tomé** (el peregrino) | A traveller who has seen other valleys fall silent. He knows it can be undone. |
| **El Rey Demonio** | Not evil for its own sake: he was once the valley's first scribe, who wanted to own every word. |

## Act 1 — The stranger with no words (levels 1–10)

**What happens.** You wake at the edge of Valdeluz. You can't read a single sign. Abuela Rosa gives you bread
and points at things, saying their names; Luna laughs at your accent. At the fountain the runes glow under your
hand and Sabia sees it. She explains (in the only English scene of the game, the "translation rune") that the
valley's words were stolen, and gives you your first task: learn the names of the village.

**Scenes.**
1. *Arrival*: the cart drops you at the north road. Abuela Rosa: "¡Hola! ¿Tienes hambre?"
2. *The fountain*: the runes light. Sabia: "Las runas te eligen."
3. *First words*: read 20 things in the village. Each one carves a small rune on the plaza terrace.
4. *The first night*: wolves howl from the forest. Inés warns you not to cross the bridge yet.

**Quests.**
- *Las primeras palabras*: read 20 signs and objects (left click).
- *El pan de cada día*: buy bread from Carmen by name; learn food words (archery, the market's el/la game).
- *Un amigo nuevo*: play Las parejas with Luna.
- *La caña de Mateo*: buy a rod from Marina and catch 3 fish (first sentences).
- *El huerto*: plant and water three crops.

**The first boss.** At level 5 Sabia sends you to the citadel's first hall. **El Guardián de Piedra** holds the
*basic words*. Beat him and the plaza's first big rune re-lights: the villagers remember the names of everyday things.

**End of the act.** **La Reina Araña** (food and animals, level 10). When she falls, the bakery's oven, cold for
years, lights by itself. Abuela Rosa cries: she remembers the word for her husband's favourite cake.

---

## Act 2 — What things are, and how they are (levels 10–20)

**What happens.** With names back, the village can talk, but badly: nobody remembers the difference between what
something *is* and how it *is right now*. The twins Sera and Estela, who used to finish each other's sentences,
have not spoken to each other in years, because each thinks the other is wrong. You train at the Academy.

**Scenes.**
1. *The twins' quarrel*: "¡La sopa ES fría!" "¡No! ¡ESTÁ fría!"
2. *Don Cortés's lesson*: the mayor visits and you must not call her "tú".
3. *The tavern reopens*: Doña Lola lights the lamps; the first bingo night in ten years.
4. *The huntress*: Inés lets you into the forest. Her brother sits by the path, silent.

**Quests.**
- *Las gemelas*: score 15 in ¿Ser o estar? and the twins make peace.
- *Buenos modales*: finish ¿Tú o usted? without a mistake.
- *Noche de bingo*: win a line at the tavern (numbers).
- *El hermano de Inés*: bring him a rune fish from the river; he says one word: "Gracias."
- *La mina*: buy a pickaxe and find a relic for Don Ernesto.

**Bosses.** **El Rey Trol** (ser or estar, level 15) sits on a throne he insists *is* his. **El Brujo de la
Niebla** (questions and replies, level 20) answers every question with fog. Beating him gives the village back
its conversations: this is when the specialists (Simón, Rosa, Benito, Pepe, Tomé) start talking with you.

---

## Act 3 — Yesterday and tomorrow (levels 20–35)

**What happens.** The villagers can talk about now, but not about the past or the future. Nobody can say what
happened to the valley, or make a plan to fix it. Bruno's forge is cold because a blade needs a verb in the
right time spoken over it. Doña Runa finally invites you into the Rune Tower.

**Scenes.**
1. *The cold forge*: Bruno: "Ayer... yo... no sé cómo se dice."
2. *The Rune Tower*: Doña Runa shows you the altar. She teaches the first spell: Chispa.
3. *The relic*: Pepe's Roman coin has writing on it in the old runes: a list of scribes. One name is scratched out.
4. *The lake*: Simón has seen lights under the water at night. Runes are washing down from the citadel.

**Quests.**
- *La forja de verbos*: forge a silver blade with Bruno (verb tenses).
- *Aprendiz de maga*: learn Chispa and Escudo de luz; enchant a weapon.
- *El nombre borrado*: find the four relics in the mine and the hills (archaeology).
- *La cosecha*: help Rosa bring in the wheat (the word harvest).
- *Las luces del lago*: catch a legendary fish at the lake.

**Bosses.** **El Rey de Hielo** (the past, level 25) is frozen in a single moment and cannot remember before it.
**La Serpiente Emplumada** (the future, level 30) promises things that never come. **El Gigante de Lava** (long
sentences, level 35) speaks in avalanches. With the past back, Abuela Rosa remembers her husband's name and
tells you their story: the emotional peak of the game.

---

## Act 4 — The scribe's name (levels 35–50)

**What happens.** The four relics together spell the scratched-out name: the Demon King was **Escribano**, the
first scribe, who believed words should belong to whoever writes them down. Doña Runa admits she was his
apprentice and helped carve the first cage. The last guardians are the hardest because they were once people.

**Scenes.**
1. *The confession*: Doña Runa: "Yo le ayudé. Perdóname."
2. *The mirror*: **La Bruja del Espejo** (translation, level 40) shows you yourself speaking perfect Spanish,
   and offers to make it true if you stop climbing.
3. *The knight*: **El Caballero Sin Nombre** (everything, level 45) is Inés's brother's captain. He gave up his
   name to keep his men alive. Beat him and say his name back to him.
4. *The village climbs*: everyone you helped comes to the citadel door and says one word each to open the last stair.

**Quests.**
- *Los cuatro hallazgos*: assemble the relics at the altar.
- *El reflejo*: refuse the Mirror Witch (a conversation built from reply tiles, with no English shown).
- *Un nombre para el caballero*: collect his name from four villagers' memories.
- *Todos juntos*: finish every specialist's conversation.

---

## Act 5 — The last word (level 50)

**The final boss.** **El Rey Demonio** does not fight; he asks. Every question is one you have answered before,
in every activity of the game, and he takes a word from the village each time you are wrong (the screen's signs
go blank). When he is beaten he asks you one last thing: "¿Qué palabra quieres darme?" The answer you build from
tiles decides the ending:

- **"Perdón"**: he becomes a scribe again and stays to re-carve what he took. Best ending; he joins the Academy.
- **"Adiós"**: he leaves the valley. The words return, but slowly.
- **"Nada"**: he is sealed in the citadel. The words return at once; Doña Runa keeps watch alone.

**After the story.** The valley speaks again. New lands open (other languages' valleys: the Japanese and Korean
packs), and Tomé offers to guide you there.

## How the story maps onto what is already built

| Story beat | Already in the game |
|---|---|
| Learning the names of things | Reading (left click), archery, pairs, the word harvest, El o la |
| Ser / estar, tú / usted | The Academy: Sera y Estela, Don Cortés |
| Past and future | The verb forge; the Ice King and the Feathered Serpent |
| Conversations returning | The six specialists with reply tiles |
| Numbers | Tavern bingo |
| The relics and the scribe's name | The mine, the hills, the four relics |
| Spells from words | The spellbook, the Rune Tower |
| Ten guardians | The ten floors of the citadel, each a unit test |

- Lines for each scene (most villagers currently have two or three lines).
- The "village loses a word" effect for the last fight.
- Per-act locks (for example, the forest path closed by Inés until Act 2).
